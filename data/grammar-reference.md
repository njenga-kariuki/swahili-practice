# Swahili Grammar Reference (Kenyan Focus)

## Verb Structure

Swahili verbs follow this pattern:
```
[Subject Prefix] + [Tense Marker] + [Object Infix (optional)] + [Verb Stem] + [Extension (optional)]
```

Example: **Ni-li-m-som-ea** = I read to/for him
- ni- (I) + -li- (past) + -m- (him) + -som- (read) + -ea (for/to)

---

## Subject Prefixes

| Person | Affirmative | Negative |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| I (mimi) | ni- | si- |
| You sg (wewe) | u- | hu- |
| He/She (yeye) | a- | ha- |
| We (sisi) | tu- | hatu- |
| You pl (ninyi) | m- | ham- |
| They (wao) | wa- | hawa- |

**Examples:**
- Ninasoma (I am reading)
- Unasoma (You are reading)
- Anasoma (He/She is reading)
- Tunasoma (We are reading)

---

## Tense Markers

### Affirmative Tenses

| Tense | Marker | Example | Meaning |
|-------|--------|---------|---------|
| Present continuous | -na- | Ninasoma | I am reading |
| Past (simple) | -li- | Nilisoma | I read (yesterday) |
| Future | -ta- | Nitasoma | I will read |
| Perfect (completed) | -me- | Nimesoma | I have read |
| Already | -sha- | Nishasoma | I have already read |
| Habitual | hu- | Husoma | I usually read |

**Note on habitual (hu-):** No subject prefix needed. "Husoma" can mean I/you/he usually reads - context determines.

### Negative Tenses

| Tense | Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|-------|---------|---------|---------|
| Present | [Neg prefix] + stem ending in -i | Sisomi | I don't read |
| Past | [Neg prefix] + -ku- + stem | Sikusoma | I didn't read |
| Future | [Neg prefix] + -ta- + stem | Sitasoma | I won't read |
| Perfect | [Neg prefix] + -ja- + stem | Sijasoma | I haven't read |

**Key pattern for present negative:** Change final -a to -i
- soma → somi
- lala → lali
- kula → kuli (irregular: "sili" for I don't eat)

---

## Object Infixes

Object infixes go BETWEEN tense marker and verb stem.

| Object | Infix | Example | Meaning |
|--------|-------|---------|---------|
| Me | -ni- | Alinionea | He saw me |
| You (sg) | -ku- | Nilikuona | I saw you |
| Him/Her | -m-/-mw- | Nilimwona | I saw him/her |
| Us | -tu- | Alituona | He saw us |
| You (pl) | -wa- | Niliwaona | I saw you all |
| Them | -wa- | Niliwaona | I saw them |

**Position:** Subject + Tense + **Object** + Verb
- Ni-li-**m**-ona = I saw him
- Tu-ta-**ku**-saidia = We will help you

## Noun Classes

### M-Wa (Class 1/2) - People

| Singular | Plural | Agreement |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| mtu (person) | watu | a-/wa- (verbs), m-/wa- (adjectives) |
| mwalimu (teacher) | walimu | huyu/hawa (this/these) |
| mtoto (child) | watoto | -angu/-angu (my) |

**Examples:**
- Mtoto **mdogo** (small child) - m- prefix on adjective
- Watoto **wadogo** (small children) - wa- prefix on adjective
- Mtu **huyu** (this person)
- Watu **hawa** (these people)

### M-Mi (Class 3/4) - Trees, Plants, Natural Objects

| Singular | Plural | Agreement |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| mti (tree) | miti | u-/i- (verbs) |
| mwili (body) | miili | m-/mi- (adjectives) |
| mkono (hand/arm) | mikono | huu/hii (this/these) |

**Examples:**
- Mti **mrefu** (tall tree) - m- prefix
- Miti **mirefu** (tall trees) - mi- prefix
- Mti **huu** (this tree)

### Ki-Vi (Class 7/8) - Objects, Tools, Diminutives

| Singular | Plural | Agreement |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| kiti (chair) | viti | ki-/vi- (verbs & adjectives) |
| kitabu (book) | vitabu | hiki/hivi (this/these) |
| chakula (food) | vyakula | ch-/vy- (possessives) |

**Examples:**
- Kiti **kikubwa** (big chair) - ki- prefix
- Viti **vikubwa** (big chairs) - vi- prefix
- Kitabu **changu** (my book) - ch- possessive
- Vitabu **vyangu** (my books) - vy- possessive

### Ji-Ma (Class 5/6) - Large/Round Objects, Fruits

| Singular | Plural | Agreement |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| jina (name) | majina | li-/ya- (verbs) |
| jicho (eye) | macho | -/ma- (adjectives, often no singular prefix) |
| embe (mango) | maembe | hili/haya (this/these) |

**Examples:**
- Jicho **kubwa** (big eye) - no prefix on adjective for Ji-
- Macho **makubwa** (big eyes) - ma- prefix
- Jina **langu** (my name) - l- possessive
- Majina **yangu** (my names) - y- possessive

### N-N (Class 9/10) - Animals, Loanwords, Misc

| Singular | Plural | Agreement |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| nyumba (house) | nyumba | i-/zi- (verbs) |
| ndizi (banana) | ndizi | n-/n- (adjectives - often same) |
| nguo (clothes) | nguo | hii/hizi (this/these) |

**Note:** Often singular and plural look identical. Context and verb agreement distinguish them.

**Examples:**
- Nyumba **nzuri** (nice house)
- Nyumba **nzuri** (nice houses) - same adjective form
- Nyumba **yangu** (my house) - y- possessive
- Nyumba **zangu** (my houses) - z- possessive

### U-N (Class 11/10) - Abstract, Some Body Parts

| Singular | Plural | Agreement |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| ukuta (wall) | kuta | u-/zi- (verbs) |
| uso (face) | nyuso | m-/n- (adjectives) |

## Possessives

Base forms: -angu (my), -ako (your), -ake (his/her), -etu (our), -enu (your pl), -ao (their)

**Must agree with noun class:**

| Class | My | Your | His/Her |
|-------|-----|------|---------|
| M-Wa | wangu | wako | wake |
| M-Mi | wangu | wako | wake |
| Ki-Vi | **changu** | **chako** | **chake** |
| Ji-Ma | **langu** | **lako** | **lake** |
| N-N | **yangu** | **yako** | **yake** |

**Examples:**
- Kitabu **changu** (my book) - Ki-vi class
- Jina **lake** (his/her name) - Ji-ma class
- Nyumba **yako** (your house) - N-n class
- Watoto **wangu** (my children) - M-wa class

## Verb Extensions

| Extension | Suffix | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|--------|---------|---------|
| Applicative | -ea/-ia | do for/to | somea (read for) |
| Reciprocal | -ana | each other | pendana (love each other) |
| Passive | -wa | be done | somwa (be read) |
| Stative | -ika/-eka | be able/possible | someka (be readable) |
| Causative | -isha/-esha | cause to do | somesha (cause to read, teach) |

**Combinations:**
- Applicative + Passive: -ewa (be done for): andikiwa (be written for)
- Causative + Reciprocal: -ishana (cause each other): funzishana (teach each other)

## Question Words

- Nani? (Who?)
- Nini? (What?)
- Wapi? (Where?)
- Lini? (When?)
- Vipi? / Namna gani? (How?)
- Kwa nini? (Why?)
- Ngapi? (How many?)
- -pi? (Which?) - agrees with noun class: yupi, kipi, lipi, etc.

## Common Patterns

### Expressing "to have"
Use "na" (with): Nina = I have (lit. "I am with")
- Nina kitabu = I have a book
- Una pesa? = Do you have money?
- Hatuna maji = We don't have water

### Expressing "to want"
-taka: Ninataka = I want
- Ninataka chai = I want tea
- Unataka nini? = What do you want?

### Expressing "to be able"
-weza: Ninaweza = I can
- Ninaweza kusoma = I can read
- Huwezi kuingia = You cannot enter

### Expressing "to like"
-penda: Ninapenda = I like
- Ninapenda kahawa = I like coffee
- Anapenda kusafiri = He/She likes to travel

## Kenyan Swahili Notes

### Common Greetings (Kenya)
- Sasa? (What's up?) → Poa / Safi / Fit
- Mambo? (How's things?) → Poa / Safi
- Habari yako? → Nzuri (sana)
- Habari za asubuhi/mchana/jioni? → Nzuri

### Polite Expressions
- Tafadhali (Please)
- Asante (sana) (Thank you very much)
- Karibu (Welcome / You're welcome)
- Pole (Sorry - for sympathy)
- Samahani (Excuse me / Sorry - for interrupting)

### Business Context
- Tutakutana... (We will meet...)
- Ningependa... (I would like...)
- Je, unaweza...? (Can you...?)
- Naomba... (I request... / May I have...)
